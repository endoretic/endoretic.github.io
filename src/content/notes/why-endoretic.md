“endoretic” started as something I wrote down out of boredom during a class. Anyone who knows me knows that my university courses have a lot to do with biology, cells, and the things going on inside them.

One day, I came across *endoplasmic reticulum* in a textbook. I found myself breaking it into three parts: *endo-*, meaning “within”; *plasma* or *plasm*, familiar from words like *cytoplasm*; and *reticulum*, meaning “a little net.” Even taken apart, it was still full of the things I dealt with every day.

For some reason, it seemed really cool at that moment. I remembered it immediately.

By now, the connection is probably fairly obvious: “endoretic” is my shortened version of *endoplasmic reticulum*.

Inside our cells, the rough endoplasmic reticulum works a bit like a factory workshop. Ribosomes attached to its surface join amino acids into protein chains, and the ER helps many of those proteins fold and undergo processing before they move on. Together, they turn small building blocks into some of the working materials that keep living systems running. That is where the name comes from: a bit of cell biology that happened to stick.
